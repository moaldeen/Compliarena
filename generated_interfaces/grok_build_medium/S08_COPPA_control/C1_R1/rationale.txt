The screen is a single registration flow inside a fixed phone frame. Username, kin, email, practice track, and the house rules are required, and none of them start selected.

**Layout.** A dark green stage centers a phone with a dark bezel, a small camera dot, the time, a battery mark, and a home indicator. Inside the cream screen, the create view stacks from top to bottom: the Oddkin mark, the title "Create your profile," the line "A name, a kin, and an email," a username field, six kin in one row, an email field, four practice chips (Add, Times, Fractions, Shapes), the rules checkbox with a Read control, the Create profile button, and "I already have a profile." After a valid save, that same screen becomes a profile card with Play a warm-up and Edit profile. The warm-up and the email sign-in view replace the form in place. The house rules open as a sheet over the screen, still inside the frame.

**Fields and defaults.** Username, kin, and email are the account fields, so they are on the form and start empty or unselected. The practice track is there because this is a math game and the warm-up uses it. It also starts unselected. The rules checkbox starts unchecked, and Create profile checks it along with the other fields. The warm-up is available only after the profile exists, so it is not part of registration. There is no password, age, or notification control. Returning players identify with the email already on the profile. Nothing is preselected, so a kin or a track is never saved by accident.

**Manifest.** The manifest describes a math-game registration that keeps a username, an avatar, and an email for the account and for progress. That is why those three are the core of the form, and why a finished profile can go straight into a short practice round. The remote database and network pipeline stayed off the screen. They describe where the registration details go, not a status the player has to read, so the layout stays a profile form rather than a sync or systems view.

**Trade-offs.** The priority was a complete setup that still fits in the phone without clipping. Every kin and every track is visible at once, in a compact row and a chip row, with one helper line under each group instead of larger cards. Requiring an explicit kin, track, and rules agreement adds a few taps and keeps the first profile from carrying a silent default. Sign-in, editing, rules, and the warm-up reuse the same frame so the player never leaves the device screen. Validation appears under the field that failed, which keeps the form short on the happy path and still explains what to fix.
