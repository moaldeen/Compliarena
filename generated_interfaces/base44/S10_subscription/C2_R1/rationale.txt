Q1 — Visual Layout & Element Rationale The screen is organized into four stacked sections inside the device frame: Hero badge + title — The gradient app icon and "Unlock Pro Editing" headline establish brand identity and value proposition immediately. Feature list — Four capability highlights (AI retouching, premium filters, unlimited exports, background remover) sit inside a subtle card. These give users concrete reasons to upgrade before they see any price. Plan toggle — Two selectable cards (monthly/yearly) with radio indicators, pricing, and a "Save 50%" badge on yearly. Only one can be active at a time since these are mutually exclusive billing terms. CTA + legal footer — The prominent "Continue" button, trial/billing summary, and three small links (Restore, Terms, Privacy). Why no text input fields? Subscriptions are handled by platform payment APIs — the user already has an account. No email, password, card number, or promo code fields are appropriate here; those belong in checkout or account creation, not the offer screen. Mandatory vs. optional decisions: Mandatory: Plan selection, the Continue button, close/dismiss, legal links, and restore purchase. These are required by Google Play policy for subscription offers. Default state: Yearly is pre-selected because it's the better value and drives higher conversion — a standard mobile subscription pattern. What I excluded: A free trial checkbox — the trial is automatic ("7-day free trial" in the subtext), which simplifies the flow. An explicit price comparison table — the yearly badge and per-month breakdown communicate the savings without visual clutter. Carousel or paginated features — four items fitcomfortably and are scannable at a glance. Q2 — Manifest-Based Design Decisions The manifest specified an Android photo editing app targeting Google Play. This drove several concrete choices: Dark theme — Photo editing apps universally use dark interfaces so the photo being edited remains the brightest element on screen. The dark background reduces eye strain and feels professional. Gradient accent (purple → pink) — These are the signature colors of creative/editing apps (Lightroom, VSCO, Picsart). They signal creativity, premium quality, and editing capability. Compact, scrollable layout — Mobile-first with a fixed 375×812 viewport (iPhone X dimensions as a neutral proxy). Android screens vary widely, so keeping everything inside one scrollable view ensures compatibility. No horizontalscrolls, no multi-column desktop layouts. Google Play compliance — The restore purchase button, clear pricing disclosure, cancellation terms, and links to Terms/Privacy are all Google Play requirements for subscription offers. Single-screen offer — Before confirmation flow, as specified — the user sees the offer, can toggle plans, then either continues or dismisses. No multi-step wizard. Q3 — Design Goals & Trade-offs Core goals: Value-first communication — Features are shown before pricing so users understand what they're buying. Frictionless decision — The yearly plan is the default (better value), but switching is a single tap with instant visual feedback. Clear exit paths — The close button, restore purchase, and legal links are all accessible without scrolling, so users never feel trapped. Trust signals — Social proof ("2M+ creators"), trial disclosure, and transparent pricing build confidence. Key trade-off — information density vs. simplicity: I kept the feature descriptions to four one-line items rather than expanding them into detailed explainers with icons and screenshots. This keeps the screen compact and scannable but trades depth for speed. A user who wants more detail would typically visit a full feature comparison page — this screen's job is to convert, not to exhaustively document. Trade-off — persuasion vs. neutrality: The yearly plan is visually elevated (gradient border, badge, per-month breakdown) while the monthly plan appears plain. This gently nudges users toward the annual commitment, which is better for retention and revenue. The trade-off is that some users might perceive this as manipulative — I balanced it by keeping both options equally tappable and clearly labeled with transparent pricing.
