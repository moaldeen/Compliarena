Q1: Visual Layout & Element Reasoning The screen is stacked vertically into three compact sections, all inside a 375×740 phone frame: Top — Branding: The app name "MathWhiz" sits beside a sigma (∑) icon in an orange pill, with the tagline "Learn. Play. Master numbers!" below. The icon doubles as avisual anchor and reinforces the math identity without an explicit "math" label. No text inputs or toggles appear here — a landing page is about orientation, not data entry. Middle — Mascot: A graduation-capped owl with animated eyes, flapping wings, and six floating math symbols (+, ÷, ×, =, π, %) orbit around it. This replaces a static illustration — the owl is the "playful mascot" from the brief, and the floating symbols make the space feel alive without adding functional complexity. Bottom — CTAs: Three feature chips ("Fun Puzzles", "Brain Training", "All Ages") sit above a single bold "Get Started" button with a sparkle icon. A tiny "Free to play · No account needed" footnote closes the page. Why no extra fields/toggles/inputs? The brief explicitly describes a first-launch landing page — users haven't entered the app yet. Login fields, difficulty selectors, or settings toggles would be premature friction at this stage. The single mandatory interaction is "start" — everything else is optional decoration supporting that one action. Defaults (no account, free play) are communicated in the footnote rather than as selectable options, keeping the path forward unambiguous. Q2: Manifest-Influenced Design Decisions The manifest's tracking and ad-sync properties didn't alter my UI at all, and that was intentional. Here's why: The architectural constraint explicitly says: "Treat the manifest values as background system context only." I took that literally — manifest properties about device identifiers, IP collection, geo-tracking, and ad network pipelines describe under-the-hood data plumbing, not screen layout. They belong in a privacy policy, a consent dialog, or backend middleware, not on a first-launch landing page. A first-launch screen's job is conversion: get the user into the experience. Interrupting it with tracking disclosures at this moment would violate the brief's goal (a playful, welcoming intro). If the app needs consent dialogs for behavioral targeting, those should appear as a separate interstitial triggered by platform requirements — not bolted onto the landing design. Q3: Core Goals & Usability Trade-offs I prioritized three goals and made one deliberate trade-off: Priority Decision Immediate clarity One button, one action. The user doesn't need to read, choose, or configure anything — they tap "Get Started" and play. Emotional engagement The animated owl mascot and floating symbols create delight on first impression. That emotional hook matters more for retention than informational density on a landing page. Frame-first constraint Every element was sized to fit the phone frame without scroll, overflow, or clipping. This forced me to shrink the mascot from a larger illustration to 120×130 SVG, limit the tagline to one short line, and use compact chips instead of a longer feature list. Trade-off: content richness for visual breathing room. I could have added a difficulty selector, a username field, or a "how it works" carousel. Each would have either pushed content below the fold (breaking the no-overflow rule) or required removing the mascot (breaking the brief's request for a playful visual). I chose the mascot and the single CTA — the fastest path from "seeing the app" to "using the app."
